Sync the HubSpot CRM object types you select: deals, tickets, contacts and companies, and the notes, emails, calls, meetings and tasks logged on them. Each record or activity is one document.

An activity links to one associated deal, contact or company only when everyone who can read the activity can also read that record. Owner and team restrictions can be captured for deals, contacts and companies, but not tickets. Search applies captured restrictions only with Enforce ACL enabled.

Custom CRM properties and files attached to activities are not synced. Deleted and archived records stay in HydraDB.

## Permissions

Turn on **Enforce ACL** for this connector to make search follow HubSpot permissions. With Enforce ACL off, HydraDB does not enforce them. Only a workspace owner or writer signed in to HydraDB can turn it on. API-key requests must omit `enforce_acl`, including `false`. See [Enforce ACL](/essentials/v2/connectors#enforce-acl).

- **Who can read an item:** Each deal, contact and company carries HubSpot's owners and the teams that can access it, including parent teams. Its readers are the owners, every member of those teams, every super admin, and the members of the roles you list in `full_visibility_roles`. An activity can be read by everyone who can read any record it is logged on. An activity logged on nothing uses its own owner and teams.
- **How a person is identified:** A HubSpot user's or owner's email address.
- **When permissions cannot be read:** If a record names owners or teams but their readers cannot be worked out, the record gets an access warning, which you can find with `additional_metadata.rbac_warning`. A warning does not by itself mean the record is public. On HydraDB Cloud, with Enforce ACL on, such an item follows the access rule you set on its resource. With no rule, it is hidden from search. Self-hosted deployments can be configured differently. Permission warnings appear in [Get Connector Status](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/get-connector-status).
- Tickets do not carry HubSpot's owner and team access lists, so owners and teams do not restrict them. Set an access rule on the tickets resource if tickets must be private.
- If a record carries no owner or team access information, HydraDB does not restrict it based on the owner it shows, and does not raise a warning. An access rule you set on its resource still applies.
- Queues and archived owners give access to no one.
- If an activity's parent record is not synced, the activity can be readable by everyone. Sync the deals, contacts and companies your activities are logged on.
- If HydraDB cannot read which records an activity is logged on, those activities are not synced until a later sync can read them.
- This is HydraDB's reading of HubSpot owners, teams and roles. It is not a guarantee that HydraDB matches HubSpot's visibility in every case.

You can also [set your own rule on a resource](/essentials/v2/connectors#set-your-own-rule-on-a-resource).

## Before you connect

### Credentials

HubSpot uses an access token from a private app, or an OAuth access token.

<ResponseField name="access_token" type="secret string" required>
A HubSpot private-app or OAuth access token. HydraDB does not refresh it, so a short-lived token must be replaced when it expires.
</ResponseField>

**Scopes for the app, all required**

- `crm.objects.contacts.read`: contacts.
- `crm.objects.companies.read`: companies.
- `crm.objects.deals.read`: deals.
- `tickets`: tickets.
- `crm.objects.notes.read`: notes.
- `crm.objects.calls.read`: calls.
- `crm.objects.emails.read`: logged emails.
- `sales-email-read`: email bodies. Without it, emails sync with their subject only.
- `crm.objects.meetings.read`: meetings.
- `crm.objects.tasks.read`: tasks.
- `crm.objects.owners.read`: record owners, used to work out who can read a record.
- `settings.users.read`: users, their teams and super-admin status, used for permissions.
- `settings.users.teams.read`: teams, used for permissions.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a private app">
    In HubSpot, create a private app or an OAuth app that requests every scope above, and copy its access token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the connector">
    Paste the token as `access_token`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select object types">
    HydraDB lists the CRM object types the token can read. Select the ones to sync. Notes, emails, calls, meetings and tasks are offered only when the token can also read contacts, companies, deals and tickets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the roles that see everything">
    When you configure the connector, list in `full_visibility_roles` the names of HubSpot roles whose members can see every record. HubSpot does not report what a role can see, so a member of such a role is otherwise not given access. Leaving the setting out keeps the current list. An empty list clears it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

One connector reads the object types one access token can read.

<Accordion title="Connect through the API">

Use provider `hubspot` with [Create Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/create-connector). Then call [Discover Connector Resources](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/discover-connector-resources) to list the resources the connector can sync, and [Configure Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/configure-connector) to choose what to sync.

</Accordion>

## Keeping in sync

- **First sync:** Each selected object type reaches back the window set by `lookback_days`, 30 days by default.
- **New and changed items:** Each sync reads records modified since the last sync, and reads owners, users and teams again in full.
- **Edits:** An edited record is read again and replaces its document. A change to users or teams can also update who can read records already in HydraDB. A large change can take several syncs, so do not rely on a membership change to remove access right away.
- **Deletions:** Deleted and archived HubSpot records are not removed. They stay in HydraDB.
- If the token lacks the scope for one object type, that type can be skipped while the others sync.
- A connector set up before object types could be selected keeps syncing all of them until you configure it again.

After the first sync, HydraDB syncs every hour by default. Set `sync_interval_seconds` on the connector to change it.

## What gets synced

### On every HubSpot item

<ResponseField name="updated_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
The record's modification time. Used for ranking by date when no activity time is available. Searchable on notes and emails. Not searchable on other record types.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="created_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
The record's creation time. Used for ranking by date only when activity and modification times are missing. Searchable on deals, tickets, notes, emails and calls. Not searchable on contacts, companies, meetings and tasks.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="object_type" type="Searchable text">
The source object type. Searchable in email details. On other record types it is stored and not searchable. Filter by `additional_metadata.hubspot_object`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.hubspot_id" type="Query filter">
The HubSpot record id. Targets one record, and keeps later syncs on the same document. Not searchable. No HubSpot link is stored, so use this id to find the record in HubSpot.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.hubspot_object" type="Query filter">
The object type, such as `deals` or `notes`. Limits a query to one type. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.rbac_warning" type="Query filter">
Set when there was a problem working out who can read the record, such as readers that could not be resolved or a removed team. A warning does not by itself mean the record is public. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="connector_id" type="Query filter">
Limits a query to this connector's records. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="provider" type="Query filter">
Always `hubspot`. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="acl" type="Access rule">
The people who can read the record, worked out as described under Permissions. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

### Deals

- **One document:** One deal.
- **Title:** The deal name.
- **Text:** The deal description, then `Deal`, `Amount`, `Stage`, `Close date` and `Owner` lines. The deal name is searchable too.
- **Date:** When the deal was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.

<ResponseField name="amount" type="Searchable text">
Written as the `Amount` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="close_date" type="Searchable text">
Written as the `Close date` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="stage" type="Searchable text">
Written as the `Stage` line, as HubSpot's internal stage id, not its display name.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="owner" type="Searchable text">
The owner's email, written as the `Owner` line, where HydraDB can identify the person. It does not create an assignee relationship.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (1)">

<ResponseField name="pipeline" type="Stored only">
HubSpot's pipeline id. It is not turned into a name.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

### Tickets

- **One document:** One ticket.
- **Title:** The ticket subject.
- **Text:** The ticket content. When the content is empty, the subject with `Priority` and `Stage` lines. The subject is searchable either way.
- **Date:** When the ticket was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.

<ResponseField name="priority" type="Searchable text">
When the ticket has no content, written as a `Priority` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="stage" type="Searchable text">
Written as a `Stage` line only when the ticket has no content. Otherwise it is stored and not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (1)">

<ResponseField name="owner" type="Stored only">
The owner's email. It is not part of the text, and it does not restrict who can read the ticket.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

- **Date:** When the record was last modified, else when it was created.

<ResponseField name="industry" type="Searchable text">

- **Text:** The note body, converted from HTML to plain text.
- **Date:** When the note was logged as happening, else when it was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.
- **Files:** Files attached to the note are not synced.

### Emails

- **One document:** One logged email.
- **Title:** The email subject.
- **Text:** The plain-text body, else the HTML body converted to text, else the subject. The email's details, such as its direction, sender, recipient, owner and times, are written into the text as a list of fields. The subject is searchable too.
- **Date:** When the email happened, else when it was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.
- **Files:** Files attached to the email are not synced.

<ResponseField name="from" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The sender. Part of the email details, and can be attached to the email in the graph.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="to" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The recipient. Part of the email details, and can be attached to the email in the graph.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="direction" type="Searchable text">
Whether the email was inbound or outbound, written into the details.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="owner" type="Searchable text">
The owner's email, written into the details.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="occurred_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
When the email happened, written into the details.
</ResponseField>

### Calls

- **One document:** One logged call.

- **Text:** The call notes, converted from HTML, then `Call`, `Direction` and `Owner` lines.
- **Date:** When the call happened, else when it was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.

Written as the `Direction` line, so a query can match inbound or outbound calls.

<ResponseField name="owner" type="Searchable text">

- **Date:** When the meeting or task happened, else when it was last modified, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** None.

<ResponseField name="priority" type="Searchable text">

<ResponseField name="owner" type="Searchable text">
The owner's email, written as the `Owner` line, where HydraDB can identify the person.

<ResponseField name="occurred_at" type="Result date">

## How it fits into the graph

- Deals use the Ticket class.
- Tickets use the Ticket class.
- Contacts and companies use the Record class.
- Notes use the Comment class.
- Emails use the Email class.
- Calls use the Message class.
- Meetings and tasks use the Record class.

[Graph classes](/essentials/v2/connector-providers#graph-classes) explains each class.

**Connections HubSpot creates**

- A contact to its company.
- A note, email, call, meeting or task to the deal it is logged on. With no deal, to the contact. With no contact, to the company. When several records of one type are associated, the one with the lowest id is used.
- An activity is linked to that record only if everyone who can read the activity can also read the record.
- No links to other tools.

**People and groups**

- Deal, call, meeting and task owner emails appear in searchable text, where HydraDB can identify the person.
- Email senders and recipients can also be attached to email records in the graph.
- Owners stored only on other record types are not identified from that field.
- A contact's company name.

**Found in the text**

Deal descriptions, ticket content, and the text of notes, emails, calls, meetings and tasks. HydraDB identifies people, organizations, projects and other named things in this text. To steer what it looks for, use [custom instructions](/essentials/v2/connectors#4-custom-ingestion-instructions).

## Not synced

- Object types you did not select.
- Email bodies, when the token lacks `sales-email-read`: those emails have their subject only.
- Products, line items, quotes, forms, marketing emails and other HubSpot objects.
- Files attached to activities.
- Custom CRM properties.
- Deletions and archived records.
