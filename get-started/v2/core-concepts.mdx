Three terms cover everything in these guides:

| Term | What it means | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Database** | A separate workspace for your data, such as `acme_corp`. A query never searches another database. Inside it, **collections** such as `company_docs` or `user_123` group related context; you name the collection on writes and reads. | [Databases and collections](/essentials/v2/multi-tenant) |
| **Context** | Everything you store. **Knowledge** is what your AI reasons over: documents such as a policy PDF, and **app sources**, which are records your code or a connector already parsed, such as a Zendesk ticket. A **memory** is something to carry between conversations: a preference, a decision, or an inferred trait. Each stored item has an `id` you use to check status, update, or delete it. | [Knowledge](/essentials/v2/knowledge), [App Sources](/essentials/v2/app-sources), [Memories](/essentials/v2/memories) |
| **Query** | A search request. It returns ranked passages of your context, and the **context graph**: relationships HydraDB extracted from your context. | [Query](/essentials/v2/query), [Context Graphs](/essentials/v2/context-graphs) |

## Choose knowledge or memory

Use **knowledge** for what your AI reasons over: files, wiki pages, tickets, and messages from apps. Use **memories** for what should carry between conversations: preferences, decisions, and inferred behavior traits.

For memories, `infer: false` (the default) stores what you send. With `infer: true`, HydraDB extracts facts and preferences from text or dialogue and checks them against what it already holds in that collection, which improves accuracy. Your application chooses what to save; HydraDB does not record interactions you have not sent it.

## Choose where the data goes

Create a database before adding context. Collections are created when you first write to them, so there is no separate collection-creation call. If you omit `collection`, HydraDB writes to and reads from the default collection only.

For most products, one collection is the right answer. Building a company brain? Put everything in one collection: connect every connector and ingest all your data there, so one query searches all of it. To keep some results away from some users, stay in that one collection and use [metadata filters](/essentials/v2/metadata), such as a team field, and [access control](/essentials/v2/access-control), the source app's permissions or your own rules. HydraDB enforces both inside a single search.

Separate collections are for data that should never meet in one query, such as one customer's memories. Send the same collection name when you search or check indexing status.

## Query

Query is how agents read from HydraDB. [`POST /query`](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/query) searches knowledge, memories, or both, chosen with `type`: `"knowledge"`, `"memory"`, or `"all"`.

HydraDB's query is a multi-stage pipeline: metadata filtering, semantic and keyword retrieval, and [context graph](/essentials/v2/context-graphs) traversal, in a single call. It returns ranked passages with their sources and the graph around them; your application puts them into the model's prompt. See [Query](/essentials/v2/query) for the request and every parameter.

A query without `acl` ignores document permissions. If your app has signed-in users, send each user's identity as `acl`, taken from their session on your backend, never from the client.

## Filter with metadata

Metadata filters are the `WHERE` clause of a query: only context whose fields match is considered, for example policies whose `status` is `approved`. Declare the fields you filter on in the database's metadata schema. Use `additional_metadata` for free-form fields such as a document's author or URL. See [Metadata](/essentials/v2/metadata) for both.
