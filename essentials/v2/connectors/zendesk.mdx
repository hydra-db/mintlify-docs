Sync Zendesk Support tickets from the brands you select, with every comment on them: public replies and internal notes. Each ticket is one document and each comment is its own document, linked to its ticket. PDF and DOCX files attached to comments are read, and their text is searchable with the comment.

A ticket assigned to a group can be restricted to that group's members, the assignee, the requester and Zendesk admins. A ticket with no assigned group carries no Zendesk permissions. Search applies these restrictions only when Enforce ACL is on. Authorize as a Zendesk administrator, or only that person's own tickets sync. Satisfaction ratings and Help Center articles are not synced.

## Permissions

Turn on **Enforce ACL** for this connector to make search follow Zendesk permissions. With Enforce ACL off, HydraDB does not enforce them. Only a workspace owner or writer signed in to HydraDB can turn it on. API-key requests must omit `enforce_acl`, including `false`. See [Enforce ACL](/essentials/v2/connectors#enforce-acl).

- **Who can read an item:** A ticket with an assigned group can be read by the group's members, the assignee, the requester and Zendesk admins. A comment can be read by its ticket's readers. The requester cannot read an internal note, unless they also have access as an admin, the assignee or a group member.
- **How a person is identified:** A Zendesk user's email address.
- **When permissions cannot be read:** If a group's memberships are missing or incomplete, a member cannot be identified, a ticket has too many readers, or a comment's ticket cannot be found, the item gets a permission warning, which you can find with `additional_metadata.rbac_warning`. On HydraDB Cloud, with Enforce ACL on, such an item follows the access rule you set on its resource. With no rule, it is hidden from search. Self-hosted deployments can be configured differently. Permission warnings appear in [Get Connector Status](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/get-connector-status).
- A ticket with no assigned group carries no Zendesk permissions and gets no warning. It follows the access rule you set on its resource. With no rule, it is visible to everyone who can query the collection, and so are its comments, including internal notes.
- Readers come from the assigned group and the people named on the ticket. Other Zendesk visibility settings are not reproduced.
- Readers are worked out when a ticket is synced. Do not rely on a change to Zendesk group membership to remove access right away.

You can also [set your own rule on a resource](/essentials/v2/connectors#set-your-own-rule-on-a-resource).

## Before you connect

### Credentials

Zendesk uses an OAuth access token, with a refresh token and the OAuth client id and secret so HydraDB can renew it. OAuth is the recommended option for a new connection. An existing Zendesk API token also works.

<ResponseField name="subdomain" type="string" required>
Your Zendesk subdomain, for example `acme` for `acme.zendesk.com`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="access_token" type="secret string">
An OAuth access token.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="refresh_token" type="secret string">
The OAuth refresh token, used to renew the access token.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="client_id" type="string">
The OAuth client id.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="client_secret" type="secret string">
The OAuth client secret.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Other credential options">

**Existing API token**

<ResponseField name="api_token" type="secret string">
A Zendesk API token. Send it with `subdomain` and `email`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="email" type="string">

</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

**What the connection needs**

- `read`: OAuth scope.
- `Administrator`: the Zendesk role of the person who authorizes. Otherwise only that person's own tickets sync.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Authorize as an administrator">
    Sign in to Zendesk as an administrator and authorize the connection with the `read` scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the connector">
    Enter `subdomain` and the OAuth token fields.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select brands">
    HydraDB lists the account's brands. Select the ones to sync. Tickets from every brand are still read from Zendesk and filtered afterwards. A comment follows its ticket's brand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

One connector reads one Zendesk subdomain.

<Accordion title="Connect through the API">

Use provider `zendesk` with [Create Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/create-connector). Then call [Discover Connector Resources](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/discover-connector-resources) to list the resources the connector can sync, and [Configure Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/configure-connector) to choose what to sync.

</Accordion>

## Keeping in sync

- **First sync:** Each selected brand reaches back the window set by `lookback_days`, 30 days by default. Comments come with the tickets in that window.
- **New and changed items:** Each sync reads tickets changed since the last sync, with their comments.
- **Edits:** A changed ticket is read again with its comments and updates their documents.
- **Deletions:** Tickets deleted in Zendesk stay searchable in HydraDB.
- Zendesk users, groups, group memberships and organizations are read for the whole account on every sync, whatever brands you select. They name people and decide who can read a ticket. They are not documents of their own.

After the first sync, HydraDB syncs every hour by default. Set `sync_interval_seconds` on the connector to change it.

## What gets synced

### On every Zendesk item

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.zendesk_id" type="Query filter">
The ticket id on a ticket, the comment id on a comment. Targets one item, and keeps later syncs on the same document. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.rbac_warning" type="Query filter">
Set when HydraDB could not fully work out who can read the item, or when a group that granted access was deleted. A warning does not by itself mean the item is public. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="connector_id" type="Query filter">
Limits a query to this connector's items. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="provider" type="Query filter">
Always `zendesk`. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="acl" type="Access rule">
The people who can read the item, worked out as described under Permissions. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

### Tickets

- **One document:** One ticket.
- **Title:** The ticket subject. A ticket without one gets a title with its ticket id.
- **Text:** The ticket description, or the subject when the description is empty. Then `Requester`, `Assignee`, `Organization` and `Group` lines. The status, priority and creation time are part of the text too. The subject is searchable too.
- **Date:** When the ticket was last updated, else when it was created.
- **Link back:** The ticket in the Zendesk agent interface.
- **Files:** None on the ticket itself. Attachments are on its comments.

<ResponseField name="status" type="Searchable text">
New, open, pending, hold, solved or closed, written as a `Status` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="priority" type="Searchable text">
Urgent, high, normal or low, written as a `Priority` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="requester" type="Searchable text">
The requester's name, written as the `Requester` line, where HydraDB can identify the customer.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="assignee" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The assigned agent's name, written as the `Assignee` line. The graph records the ticket as assigned to that agent.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="organization" type="Searchable text">
The organization's name, written as the `Organization` line, where HydraDB can identify the company.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="group" type="Searchable text">
The assigned group's name, written as the `Group` line. Who can read the ticket is worked out from the group's members, not from this name.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="created_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
When the ticket was created, written as a `Created` line. Used as the result date only when the update time is missing.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="updated_at" type="Result date">
When the ticket was last updated. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (3)">

<ResponseField name="type" type="Stored only">
Problem, incident, question or task.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="tags" type="Stored only">
The ticket's tags. They are not part of the searchable text.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="api_url" type="Stored only">
The ticket's address in the Zendesk API. The link back is the agent interface address.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

### Comments

- **One document:** One comment on a ticket: a public reply or an internal note.
- **Title:** `Comment by` and the author's name. Without an author, the first 80 characters of the comment.
- **Text:** An `Author` line, then the plain text of the comment, or `Attachment:` and the file name for a comment that has only an attachment. The ticket id and the creation time are part of the text too.
- **Date:** When the comment was created.
- **Link back:** None. Open the ticket from its own document.
- **Files:** PDF and DOCX attachments up to 25 MiB are downloaded and read, and their text is searchable with the comment. An attachment that fails HydraDB's checks is skipped. Documents in other formats are not read.

<ResponseField name="author" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The name of the person who wrote the comment, written as the `Author` line. The graph records the comment as written by that person.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="mentions" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
Names @mentioned in an internal note. The names stay in the note text, and the graph records each as mentioned.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="parent_id" type="Searchable text, Graph connection">
The ticket the comment belongs to, written into the text as the ticket id. Links the comment to its ticket. The ticket's text is read together with the comment.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.zendesk_ticket_id" type="Query filter">
The id of the ticket the comment belongs to. Returns every comment on one ticket. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="created_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
When the comment was created, written as a `Created` line.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="public" type="Access rule">
True for a public reply, false for an internal note. The requester cannot read an internal note through being the requester. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (1)">

<ResponseField name="author_id" type="Stored only">
The Zendesk user id of the comment's author.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

**Example searches**

- What did we tell Acme about the export bug?
- Which urgent tickets are open for the billing group?
- What did an agent write on ticket 123?

## How it fits into the graph

- Tickets use the Ticket class.
- Comments use the Comment class.

[Graph classes](/essentials/v2/connector-providers#graph-classes) explains each class.

**Connections Zendesk creates**

- A comment to its ticket.
- No links to other tools.

**People and groups**

- The assigned agent of each ticket.
- The author of each comment.
- Names @mentioned in an internal note.
- The requester, the organization and the group appear in the ticket text, where HydraDB can identify them.

**Found in the text**

The ticket description with its requester, assignee, organization and group lines, the comment text, and the text of attached PDF and DOCX files. Each comment is read together with its ticket's text. HydraDB identifies people, organizations, projects and other named things in this text. To steer what it looks for, use [custom instructions](/essentials/v2/connectors#4-custom-ingestion-instructions).

## Not synced

- Brands you did not select.
- Satisfaction ratings.
- Help Center articles and community posts.
- Ticket audits, macros, ticket fields and forms, SLA policies and metric events.
- Ticket custom fields.
- Attached documents in formats other than PDF and DOCX.
