HydraDB stores the context your AI application needs and returns the relevant parts when you ask. You send it documents, app data, and what you learn about each user. Your application queries it and puts the results in the model's prompt. The model writes the answer; HydraDB finds what to show it.

A vector database finds what is similar. Your agent needs what is useful to the person asking: the sales deck for the account executive, the changelog for the engineer, and what this customer already tried for the support bot. HydraDB keeps shared knowledge, per-user memory, and the relationships between them in one place, so one query returns the slice of context that fits the question and the user.

## What it stores

- **Knowledge:** the material your agent answers from. Upload files (PDF, DOCX, Markdown, CSV, TXT) or send records from apps such as Slack, Jira, Notion, and Gmail. See [Knowledge](/essentials/v2/knowledge).
- **Memories:** what your agent learns about one user: preferences, decisions, past conversations. Your application saves each memory in that user's collection. See [Memories](/essentials/v2/memories).

Everything lives in a **database** you create, organized into **collections** for each customer, team, or user. Ingestion runs in the background; you poll a status endpoint or receive a webhook when content is searchable. [Core Concepts](/get-started/v2/core-concepts) defines these terms in five minutes.

## How you get it back

One endpoint, [`POST /query`](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/query), searches knowledge, memories, or both. It combines search by meaning with keyword matching (hybrid search) and returns the matching passages with their source details. It also returns a [context graph](/essentials/v2/context-graphs): relationships HydraDB extracted from your content, such as "the Payments team owns the billing service", so the model sees how the passages connect.

Your application chooses the database and collections to search and tells HydraDB who is asking. HydraDB does not know your users unless you tell it. The defaults work without tuning; `alpha`, `mode`, `recency_bias`, and `graph_context` are yours to change. Your context. Your opinions.

## What the product includes

- **Connectors:** 70 providers that sync an app into your database on a schedule, including Slack, Gmail, Google Drive, Notion, GitHub, Jira, Confluence, Linear, HubSpot, Zendesk, and Microsoft Teams. See [Connectors](/essentials/v2/connectors).
- **Access control:** a per-document list of who may retrieve it, declared by you or captured from the source app, enforced on each query. See [Access Control](/essentials/v2/access-control).
- **Webhooks:** an HTTP callback when a document or memory finishes indexing. See [Webhooks](/essentials/v2/webhooks).
- **SDKs:** [Python and TypeScript](/api-reference/v2/sdks), with a helper that turns a query result into a prompt string.
- **Tools:** an [MCP server](/plugins/mcp) for Claude, Cursor, and other MCP clients, a [CLI](/plugins/cli), and plugins for [Claude Code](/plugins/claude-code) and [OpenClaw](/plugins/openclaw).

## Performance

- **Memory recall:** 90.79% on LongMemEval-S, a benchmark for remembering information from past conversations.
- **Low latency:** sub-200ms retrieval latency.

View the full technical breakdown in our [benchmarks](https://benchmarks.hydradb.com/).

## What you can build
