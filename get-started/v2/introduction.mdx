## What HydraDB is

HydraDB is a context layer for AI applications. It stores two types of context:

- **Knowledge:** documents, content synced from your apps, and facts your AI reasons over.
- **Memories:** preferences, decisions, and past conversations that persist across sessions.

Everything lives in a **database** you create. You ingest context and connect the tools your team already uses; HydraDB indexes it in the background and builds a context graph from it. It is serverless: you never provision, shard, or scale anything, and the same code runs at ten documents or ten million.

The [Quickstart](/get-started/v2/quickstart) takes you from an empty database to your first query in five minutes.

## The problem we're solving

A vector database finds what is similar. An agent needs what is useful, and a useful answer may depend on who made a decision, how a project changed, and who is asking. A sales rep searching for "project Acme" needs the latest sales deck; an engineer needs the changelog and architecture decisions. Similarity alone does not account for those differences.

To handle this, teams often combine a vector database, a graph database, a relational store, and a cache, then build the connections between them, keep data in sync, and maintain the retrieval logic themselves.

HydraDB keeps your knowledge, your memories, and the relationships between them in one graph, so your agent can retrieve the context that fits the person asking.

## The principle

HydraDB gives you primitives, not a fixed product. The graph, the memory primitives, the retrieval pipeline, and the ranking controls are yours to compose into your own context stores, memory layers, and workflows.

## Performance

- **Memory recall:** 90.79% on [LongMemEval-S](https://huggingface.co/datasets/xiaowu0162/longmemeval-cleaned).
- **Company knowledge:** 88.7 overall on [EnterpriseRAG-Bench](https://github.com/onyx-dot-app/EnterpriseRAG-Bench), retrieval across more than half a million documents from nine enterprise systems.
- **Coding harness:** 90.73 on [CodeWikiBench](https://github.com/FSoft-AI4Code/CodeWikiBench) for nlohmann/json, against 66.06 for DeepWiki and 61.28 for CodeWiki.
- **Long-term memory at scale:** 82% overall on [BEAM 1M](https://github.com/mohammadtavakoli78/BEAM), across ten dimensions.
- **Financial AI:** 91.4% recall@10 on [FinanceBench](https://github.com/patronus-ai/financebench), over dense filings and enterprise data.
- **Low latency:** sub-200 ms retrieval.

The full technical breakdown of each run is in our [benchmarks](https://benchmarks.hydradb.com/).

## More in the box

- **Connectors:** 70 providers, including Slack, Gmail, Google Drive, Notion, GitHub, Jira, Confluence, Linear, HubSpot, Zendesk, and Microsoft Teams, synced on a schedule. Where the provider supports it, the source app's permissions come along as access control, so a query that sends a user's identity as `acl` returns only what that user may see. See [Connectors](/essentials/v2/connectors) and [Query](/essentials/v2/query#6-query-on-behalf-of-a-user).
- **SDKs:** [Python and TypeScript](/api-reference/v2/sdks), with a helper that turns a query result into a prompt string.
- **Tools:** an [MCP server](/plugins/mcp) for Claude, Cursor, and other MCP clients; a [CLI](/plugins/cli); and memory plugins for coding agents and agent frameworks such as [Claude Code](/plugins/claude-code), [Codex](/plugins/codex), [Hermes](/plugins/hermes), and [OpenClaw](/plugins/openclaw).

## What you can build

- Customer support agents grounded in real customer history and preferences
- Coding agents with persistent memory of a codebase and team conventions
- Clinical companions tracking patient context across visits
- Research copilots reasoning across papers, authors, and findings
- Internal knowledge assistants spanning Slack, Notion, Drive, and email
- Consumer AI applications that remember each user across sessions

That is a sample, not the limit.
