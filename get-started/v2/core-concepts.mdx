Three terms cover everything in these guides:

| Term | What it means | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Database** | A separate workspace for your data, such as `acme_corp`. A query never searches another database. Inside it, **collections** such as `company_docs` or `user_123` group related content; you name the collection on writes and reads. | [Databases and collections](/essentials/v2/multi-tenant) |
| **Context** | Everything you store. **Knowledge** is what your AI reasons over: documents such as a policy PDF, and **app sources**: records your code or a connector already parsed, such as a Zendesk ticket. A **memory** is something to carry between conversations: a preference, a decision, or an inferred trait. Each stored item has an `id` you use to check status, update, or delete it. | [Knowledge](/essentials/v2/knowledge), [App Sources](/essentials/v2/app-sources), [Memories](/essentials/v2/memories) |
| **Query** | A search request. It returns ranked passages of your context, and the **context graph**: relationships HydraDB extracted from the content. | [Query](/essentials/v2/query), [Context Graphs](/essentials/v2/context-graphs) |

## Choose knowledge or memory

Use **knowledge** for what your AI reasons over: files, wiki pages, tickets, and messages from apps. Use **memories** for what should carry between conversations: preferences, decisions, and inferred behavior traits.

For memories, `infer: false` (the default) stores what you send. With `infer: true`, HydraDB extracts facts and preferences from text or dialogue and checks them against what it already holds in that collection, which improves accuracy. Your application chooses what to save; HydraDB does not record interactions you have not sent it.

## Choose where the data goes

Create a database before adding content. Collections are created when you first write to them, so there is no separate collection-creation call. If you omit `collection`, HydraDB uses the database's default collection, not every collection.

For most products, one collection is the right answer. Building a company brain? Put everything in a single collection: connect all your connectors and ingest all your data there, so one query can search across it. When a search must span all the data but some results must stay out of reach for some users, keep the single collection and use [metadata](/essentials/v2/metadata) filters and [access control](/essentials/v2/access-control) for the isolation: a user id or a team as a metadata field, and the source app's permissions, or your own, as the ACL. HydraDB then enforces who sees what inside one search.

Separate collections are for data that should never meet in one query, such as one customer's memories. Send the same collection name when you search or check indexing status.

## Query

Query is how agents read from HydraDB. [`POST /query`](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/query) searches knowledge, memories, or both, chosen with `type`: `"knowledge"`, `"memory"`, or `"all"`.

HydraDB's query is a multi-stage pipeline: metadata filtering, semantic and keyword retrieval, and [context graph](/essentials/v2/context-graphs) traversal, in a single call. It returns ranked passages with their sources and the graph around them; your application puts them into the model's prompt. The [Query](/essentials/v2/query) guide has the request and every parameter.

Send the signed-in user's identity as `acl` on every query, derived by your backend from the session, never from an identity the client supplies. A query without `acl` does not enforce document permissions.

## Filter with metadata

Metadata filters are the `WHERE` clause of a query: only content whose fields match is considered, for example policies whose `status` is `approved`. Declare the fields you filter on in the database's metadata schema. Use `additional_metadata` for free-form fields such as a document's author or URL. The [Metadata guide](/essentials/v2/metadata) shows both.
