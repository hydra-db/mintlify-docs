Sync the Notion databases and top-level pages you share with the connector's integration. Each page is one document: a database row, a selected page, and every sub-page under them. Each sub-page links to the page it sits under.

A database row brings in its title and page text only. Status, owner, dates, tags and other properties are not read. Notion page sharing is not imported, so every synced page is visible to everyone who can query the collection unless you set an access rule. Pages deleted or unshared in Notion stay in HydraDB.

## Permissions

<Warning>
Notion permissions are not imported, so **Enforce ACL** is unavailable for this connector. Every synced item is visible to everyone who can query the collection, unless you [set an access rule on the resource](/essentials/v2/connectors#set-your-own-rule-on-a-resource).
</Warning>

API-key requests must omit `enforce_acl`, including `false`. See [ACL on synced content](/essentials/v2/connectors#5-acl-native-rbac-awareness).

## Before you connect

### Credentials

Notion uses an internal integration token.

<ResponseField name="access_token" type="secret string" required>
A Notion internal integration token. It starts with `secret_` or `ntn_`.
</ResponseField>

**Capabilities to give the integration**

- `Read content`: required. Lets HydraDB search, read pages and blocks, and query databases.
- `Read user information without email`: optional. Lets HydraDB check the token and show who created a page.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an integration">
    In Notion, create an internal integration with the Read content capability, and copy its token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share content with it">
    Share each database and page you want synced with the integration. HydraDB can list only what the integration can see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the connector">
    Paste the token as `access_token`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select resources">
    HydraDB lists every database the integration can see, and every page that sits at the top of the workspace. Select at least one database or top-level page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

One connector reads what one integration can see.

To connect more than one Notion workspace, give each connector its own `provider_account_scope`, such as `notion-account-a` and `notion-account-b`. These are labels you choose, not Notion's own account ids, and HydraDB does not check them. Two connectors that share a scope and write to the same collection can overwrite each other's items. See [several accounts from one provider](/essentials/v2/connectors#6-connect-several-accounts-from-one-provider).

<Accordion title="Connect through the API">

Use provider `notion` with [Create Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/create-connector). Then call [Discover Connector Resources](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/discover-connector-resources) to list what the credentials can reach, and [Configure Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/configure-connector) to choose what to sync.

</Accordion>

## Keeping in sync

- **First sync:** A database reaches back 30 days by last edited time by default, set by `lookback_days`. A selected page and its sub-pages are read again on every sync.
- **New and changed items:** For a database, rows edited since the last sync are read again in full, with their sub-pages. For a selected page, the whole page tree is read on every sync, and the pages edited since the last sync are sent.
- **Edits:** For a directly selected page tree, edited pages replace their existing documents on the next sync. For a sub-page under a database row, the change is picked up only when the database row is also edited.
- **Deletions:** Pages deleted or unshared in Notion are not removed. They stay searchable in HydraDB.
- Sub-pages are followed up to 10 levels deep, and blocks up to 20 levels deep.
- If a sub-page or block is unavailable, its content is omitted. Other accessible content on the page can still sync.

After the first sync, HydraDB syncs every hour by default. Set `sync_interval_seconds` on the connector to change it.

## What gets synced

### Pages

- **One document:** One Notion page: a database row, a selected page, or any sub-page under them. A sub-page is its own document, not part of its parent's text.
- **Title:** The page's title. A page without a title uses its Notion id.
- **Text:** The page title at the top, the creator as an `Author` line, and the plain text of the page's blocks in order, one block per line: paragraphs, headings, bulleted and numbered lists, to-dos, toggles, quotes, callouts and code. Text inside toggles and callouts is included.
- **Date:** When the page was last edited.
- **Link back:** The page in Notion.
- **Files:** Images and files remain links to the originals. Image text is searchable only when image reading is available in your deployment. Do not rely on PDF or Word attachment text being searchable.

<ResponseField name="created_by" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The creator's display name, or their Notion id if the name cannot be read. It is the `Author` line of the text, and the graph records the page as written by that person.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="last_edited_time" type="Result date">
When the page was last edited. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.notion_page_id" type="Query filter">
The page's Notion id. Limits a query to one page. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="connector_id" type="Query filter">
Limits a query to this connector's pages. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="provider" type="Query filter">
Always `notion`. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="parent_id" type="Graph connection">
For a sub-page, the page it sits under. Links the two, so a page tree is connected in the graph. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="created_by_email" type="Graph identity">
The creator's email, when Notion returns one. It lets the graph recognise the same person across tools. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="mentions" type="Graph identity">
People @mentioned in the page. The graph records each as mentioned by the page. The field itself is not searchable, but the page text it comes from is.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (2)">

<ResponseField name="created_at" type="Stored only">
When the page was created.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="last_edited_by" type="Stored only">
The Notion user id of the last editor. It is not turned into a name or a person.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

**Example searches**

- What does our onboarding page say about laptop setup?
- Which Notion pages did Priya create?
- What sub-pages sit under the Engineering Handbook page?

## How it fits into the graph

- Pages use the Page class.

[Graph classes](/essentials/v2/connector-providers#graph-classes) explains each class.

**Connections Notion creates**

- A sub-page to the page it sits under.
- No links from Notion pages to other tools. In the other direction, GitHub issues, pull requests and comments that link to a Notion page are linked to it.

**People and groups**

- The page's creator, with an email when Notion returns one.
- People @mentioned in the page.

**Found in the text**

The page text, including people and projects named in plain text rather than @mentioned. Database properties are not read, so anything kept only in database columns cannot be found. HydraDB identifies people, organizations, projects and other named things in this text. To steer what it looks for, use [custom instructions](/essentials/v2/connectors#4-custom-ingestion-instructions).

## Not synced

- Property values other than the title, such as status, owner, dates and tags, are not read.
- Comments on pages.
- Pages and databases not shared with the integration.
- Text in unsupported blocks, such as table rows, equations, embeds and bookmarks.
- Deletions: deleted pages stay in HydraDB.
