Suppose you are building a support assistant. It needs your refund policy to answer questions and a customer's previous support history to avoid suggesting a fix they already tried. HydraDB stores both; your application retrieves them and gives them to the model answering the customer.

Three terms cover everything the guides use:

| Term | What it means in this example | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Database** | A separate workspace for your data, such as `acme_corp`. A query never searches another database. Inside it, **collections** such as `company_docs` or `user_123` group related content; you name the collection on writes and reads. | [Databases and collections](/essentials/v2/multi-tenant) |
| **Context** | Everything you store. **Knowledge** is the documents, app sources, and facts your AI reasons over: the refund policy PDF, or an **app source** such as the customer's Zendesk ticket, a record your code or a connector already parsed. A **memory** is something to remember: "this customer already restarted the app." Each stored item has an `id` you use to check status, update, or delete it. | [Knowledge](/essentials/v2/knowledge), [App Sources](/essentials/v2/app-sources), [Memories](/essentials/v2/memories) |
| **Query** | A search request, such as "What should this customer try next?" It returns ranked passages of your context, and the **context graph**: relationships HydraDB extracted from the content, such as "the refund policy covers annual plans." | [Query](/essentials/v2/query), [Context Graphs](/essentials/v2/context-graphs) |

## Choose knowledge or memory

Use **knowledge** for source material your agent should consult: files, wiki pages, tickets, or messages from apps. Use **memories** for information you want to carry between conversations: preferences, decisions, or what happened previously.

These are content categories, not permissions. A private document is still knowledge, and a memory stays with one person only when your application stores and searches it in that person's collection.

For memories, `infer: false` (the default) stores what you send. With `infer: true`, HydraDB extracts useful facts or preferences from text or dialogue. Your application chooses what to save; HydraDB does not record interactions you have not sent it.

## Choose where the data goes

Create a database before adding content. Collections are created when you first write to them, so there is no separate collection-creation call. If you omit `collection`, HydraDB uses the database's default collection, not every collection.

For most products, one collection is the right answer. Building a company brain? Put everything in a single collection: connect all your connectors and ingest all your data there, so one query can search across it. When a search must span all the data but some results must stay out of reach for some users, keep the single collection and use [metadata](/essentials/v2/metadata) filters and [access control](/essentials/v2/access-control) for the isolation: a user id or a team as a metadata field, and the source app's permissions, or your own, as the ACL. HydraDB then enforces who sees what inside one search.

Separate collections are for data that should never meet in one query, such as the per-customer memories of the support assistant. Keep using the same names when you search or check indexing status.

## Query

[`POST /query`](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/query) searches knowledge, memories, or both, chosen with `type`: `"knowledge"`, `"memory"`, or `"all"`. It returns ranked passages with their source details and the context graph for them; your application puts them into the model's prompt, and the model writes the answer. For the support assistant, one `type: "all"` request over `company_docs` and `user_123` returns the shared policies and that customer's history together. The [Query](/essentials/v2/query) guide has the request and every parameter.

Your backend must derive `acl` from the signed-in user, not trust identities supplied by the client. Queries without `acl` do not enforce document permissions; documents without an ACL remain unrestricted.

## Filter with metadata

Use metadata when a result must meet a rule: for example, only policies whose `status` is `approved`. Declare fields you use often in the database's metadata schema (the field names and types you allow). Use `additional_metadata` for free-form fields such as a document's author or external URL. The [Metadata guide](/essentials/v2/metadata) shows both storage and filter examples.
