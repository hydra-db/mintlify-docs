Sync emails from the Gmail labels you select in one Gmail account. Each email is one document. A reply links to the email it answers, and earlier emails of its thread are fetched too, even from outside the selected label. Supported attachments are read, and their text is searchable with the email.

Gmail connects with an App Password, not OAuth. Gmail permissions are not imported, so every synced email is visible to everyone who can query the collection unless you set an access rule on its label. Emails deleted or moved out of a label stay in HydraDB, and label, read and starred changes are not picked up.

## Permissions

<Warning>
Gmail permissions are not imported, so **Enforce ACL** is unavailable for this connector. Every synced item is visible to everyone who can query the collection, unless you [set an access rule on the resource](/essentials/v2/connectors#set-your-own-rule-on-a-resource).
</Warning>

- A mailbox is personal in Gmail, but HydraDB does not limit its emails to the mailbox owner. Set an access rule on each label that must stay private.
- Earlier thread emails fetched from All Mail follow the access rule of the label that brought them in, even when they sit outside that label.

API-key requests must omit `enforce_acl`, including `false`. See [ACL on synced content](/essentials/v2/connectors#5-acl-native-rbac-awareness).

## Before you connect

### Credentials

Gmail connects over IMAP with your Gmail address and a Google App Password. It does not use your Google password or OAuth.

<ResponseField name="access_token" type="secret string" required>
Your Gmail address and the App Password joined by a colon, as one string: `address:app_password`. It is a single field, not two.
</ResponseField>

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on 2-Step Verification">
    Turn on 2-Step Verification for the Google account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create an App Password">
    Generate an App Password for the Google account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the connector">
    Paste the Gmail address, a colon and the App Password as `access_token`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select labels">
    HydraDB lists every selectable mailbox of the account, such as `INBOX` and your own labels. Select the labels to sync.
  </Step>
</Steps>

One connector reads one Gmail account.

<Accordion title="Connect through the API">

Use provider `gmail` with [Create Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/create-connector). Then call [Discover Connector Resources](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/discover-connector-resources) to list the resources the connector can sync, and [Configure Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/configure-connector) to choose what to sync.

</Accordion>

## Keeping in sync

- **First sync:** Each label reaches back the window set by `lookback_days`. Earlier emails of a new email's thread are also fetched, up to 10 levels back. When they are outside the label or the lookback window, they are looked up in All Mail.
- **New and changed items:** Each sync fetches the emails that arrived in the label since the last sync. Mail moved into a selected label is fetched too when it gets a new IMAP UID, even with an old delivery date.
- **Edits:** An email that is already synced is not read again. Label, read and starred changes are not picked up.
- **Deletions:** Deleted emails are not removed. An email deleted or moved out of the label stays searchable in HydraDB.
- If Gmail resets a mailbox's message numbering, the label is read again from its lookback window.

After the first sync, HydraDB syncs every hour by default. Set `sync_interval_seconds` on the connector to change it.

## What gets synced

### Emails

- **One document:** One email. An email without a Message-ID is identified by its IMAP UID instead.
- **Title:** The email subject.
- **Text:** The subject on the first line. Then each non-empty field of the email as a labelled line, sorted by field name: the body, sender, To and CC addresses, Reply-To, delivery time, the account address, the Message-ID, the Message-ID it replies to, the conversation id and the IMAP UID. The body is plain text: HTML is converted and encoded text is decoded. An email with no body uses its subject. The subject is searchable too.
- **Date:** When the email was delivered, or its Date header when the delivery time is missing.
- **Link back:** None.
- **Files:** Named files inside a multipart email are fetched again and read. Their text becomes searchable when the file type is supported and the fetch succeeds. A file sent as the whole email, not inside a multipart email, is not read as an attachment.

<ResponseField name="from" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The sender's address, written as a line of the text. The graph records the email as sent by that address.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="to" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The To addresses, written as a line of the text. The graph records each address as a recipient.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="cc" type="Searchable text, Graph identity">
The CC addresses, written as a line of the text. The graph records each address as copied.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="reply_to" type="Searchable text">
The Reply-To addresses, written as a line of the text. They are not added to the graph as people.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="created_at" type="Searchable text, Result date">
The delivery time, written as a line of the text.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="message_id" type="Searchable text">
The email's Message-ID, written as a line of the text. It identifies the email across syncs.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="in_reply_to" type="Searchable text, Graph connection">
The Message-ID of the email this one replies to, from the In-Reply-To header or the last References entry. Links the reply to that email.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="forwarded_from_id" type="Searchable text, Graph connection">
The Message-ID of the forwarded email inside this one, when it holds exactly one. Links the email to it.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="gmail_msg_id" type="Searchable text">
The email's IMAP UID in the mailbox it was read from, which is All Mail for earlier thread emails. It is not a Gmail API message id.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.thread_id" type="Searchable text, Query filter, Graph connection">
The conversation id: the first Message-ID in the References header, else In-Reply-To, else the email's own Message-ID. Limits a query to one conversation, and groups the conversation's emails in the graph. Also written as a line of the text.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.account_email" type="Searchable text, Query filter">
The Gmail account address. Limits a query to emails from one account. Also written as a line of the text.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="connector_id" type="Query filter">
Limits a query to this connector's emails. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="provider" type="Query filter">
Always `gmail`. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Notification links" type="Graph connection">
Links a GitHub or Linear notification email to the GitHub issue or pull request, or the Linear issue, it is about. Only emails from `notifications@github.com`, `notifications@linear.app` or `notifications@mail.linear.app` that pass Gmail's DKIM check are linked. At most 32 links per email. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Label" type="Access rule">
The selected label the email was synced through. An access rule you set on the label governs its emails, including earlier thread emails fetched from All Mail. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

**Example searches**

- What did the customer say in their last email about the renewal?
- Who was copied on the contract thread with Acme?
- Which emails were about GitHub pull request 123 in our repo?

## How it fits into the graph

- Emails use the Email class.

[Graph classes](/essentials/v2/connector-providers#graph-classes) explains each class.

**Connections Gmail creates**

- A reply to the email it answers, by Message-ID.
- Every email to its conversation.
- An email to each forwarded email inside it.
- A GitHub or Linear notification email to the GitHub issue or pull request, or Linear issue, it is about. GitHub items are found from the email's headers. Linear issues are found from links in the email text.
- No other links to other tools.

**People and groups**

- The sender.
- The To and CC recipients.
- Reply-To addresses and the account address are not attached as people. BCC recipients are not read.

**Found in the text**

The subject, the body and the text read from attachments. HydraDB identifies people, organizations, projects and other named things in this text. To steer what it looks for, use [custom instructions](/essentials/v2/connectors#4-custom-ingestion-instructions).

## Not synced

- Labels you did not select, and mailboxes Gmail marks as not selectable.
- Gmail label assignments, and read and starred state.
- Email older than the first sync's lookback window, unless it is an earlier email of a synced thread.
- Deletions: emails deleted or moved out of a label stay in HydraDB.
- BCC recipients.
- A file sent as the whole email rather than as an attachment inside it.
