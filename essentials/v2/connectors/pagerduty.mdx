Sync incidents from the PagerDuty services you select, plus every schedule and escalation policy in the account. Each incident, schedule and escalation policy is one document. An incident carries its alert summaries, timeline and, by default, its notes and status updates.

Incidents follow who can read their service, and schedules and policies follow their owning teams, only when Enforce ACL is on. Schedules keep rotation layers but no dated shifts or overrides, so they cannot tell you who is on call at a given time. Incidents deleted in PagerDuty stay in HydraDB.

## Permissions

Turn on **Enforce ACL** for this connector to make search follow PagerDuty permissions. With Enforce ACL off, HydraDB does not enforce them. Only a workspace owner or writer signed in to HydraDB can turn it on. API-key requests must omit `enforce_acl`, including `false`. See [Enforce ACL](/essentials/v2/connectors#enforce-acl).

- **Who can read an item:** An incident follows the readers of the service it belongs to. A schedule or escalation policy has its own readers, worked out from its owning teams and each user's role. Account owners and admins can read everything. If an owning team is public, every user can read the item. Otherwise users with a full role read their own teams' items, and managers, responders, observers and full stakeholders also read items that belong to no team. Limited stakeholders get no access to private or teamless items.
- **How a person is identified:** A user's email address. Teams are not used as groups.
- **When permissions cannot be read:** If HydraDB cannot work out who can read a service, schedule or escalation policy, the connector reports a permission warning. On HydraDB Cloud, with Enforce ACL on, such an item follows the access rule you set on its resource. With no rule, it is hidden from search. Self-hosted deployments can be configured differently. Permission warnings appear in [Get Connector Status](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/get-connector-status).
- An item with no readers, a reader without an email, or more than 1,000 readers cannot be captured. The same applies when a user's role is one HydraDB does not recognise and no owning team is public.
- A team with no default role recorded is treated as members only.
- Roles given on single objects to restricted-access users are not read, so those users cannot see the item.
- Schedule and policy readers are checked again on every sync. If the list cannot be read, nothing is changed and the next sync tries again.

You can also [set your own rule on a resource](/essentials/v2/connectors#set-your-own-rule-on-a-resource).

## Before you connect

### Credentials

PagerDuty uses a read-only REST API key created at the account level, plus the email of a user in that account.

<ResponseField name="token" type="secret string" required>
A read-only REST API key created at the account level. A user-scoped key cannot list services, so there is nothing to select.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="email" type="string" required>
The email of a user in the PagerDuty account the key belongs to.
</ResponseField>

**Connector settings**

<ResponseField name="fetch_incident_prose" type="boolean">
On by default. When on, incident notes and status updates are synced with each incident.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="since" type="string">
An earliest incident date. Normal syncs do not use it. They use the history window instead.
</ResponseField>

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an API key">
    In PagerDuty, open Integrations, API Access Keys, Create New API Key, and tick Read-only. Create it at the account level, not for a single user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the connector">
    Paste the key as `token` and the email of a user in that account as `email`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select services">
    HydraDB lists the services the key can see. Select the services whose incidents to sync. Schedules and escalation policies are read for the whole account, whichever services you select.
  </Step>
</Steps>

One connector reads one PagerDuty account.

<Accordion title="Connect through the API">

Use provider `pagerduty` with [Create Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/create-connector). Then call [Discover Connector Resources](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/discover-connector-resources) to list the resources the connector can sync, and [Configure Connector](/api-reference/v2/endpoint/configure-connector) to choose what to sync.

</Accordion>

## Keeping in sync

- **First sync:** Incidents of each selected service reach back the window set by `lookback_days`, 30 days by default, measured by their last status change. Schedules and escalation policies are read in full.
- **New and changed items:** Each sync reads incidents whose last status change is newer than the last sync, including older incidents that changed. Schedules and escalation policies are read in full on every sync.
- **Edits:** An incident is read again when its status changes. A new note or other edit without a status change may not be picked up.
- **Deletions:** Incidents, schedules and policies deleted in PagerDuty stay searchable in HydraDB. With Enforce ACL on, a schedule or policy that no longer exists can be hidden from search, but its content is not deleted.
- Schedules and escalation policies are skipped if your PagerDuty plan or the key's permissions refuse them.

After the first sync, HydraDB syncs every hour by default. Set `sync_interval_seconds` on the connector to change it.

## What gets synced

### On every PagerDuty item

<ResponseField name="connector_id" type="Query filter">
Limits a query to this connector's items. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="provider" type="Query filter">
Always `pagerduty`. Not searchable.

### Incidents

- **One document:** One incident, with its alerts, notes, status updates and timeline.
- **Title:** The incident title, else its summary, else `PagerDuty incident` with its id.
- **Text:** `Status`, `Urgency`, `Priority`, `Service`, `Assigned To` and `Escalation Policy` lines. Then each alert's summary with its severity and context, the notes and status updates with their authors when `fetch_incident_prose` is on, and a timeline of log entry summaries and trigger descriptions in time order. Empty entries and most other alert details are left out. The title and the incident id are searchable too.
- **Date:** The last status change, else when the incident was created.
- **Link back:** The incident page in PagerDuty.

</ResponseField>

### Schedules

- **One document:** One on-call schedule. Rotation layers are included only when PagerDuty returns them with the schedule.
- **Title:** The schedule name, else its summary, else a generated title with its id.
- **Text:** The name, `Description` and `Time Zone` lines, one `Layer` line per rotation layer with its turn length and the people on it, and a `Used By` line with the escalation policies that use it. The title is searchable too.
- **Date:** None.
- **Link back:** The schedule page in PagerDuty.

<ResponseField name="users" type="Searchable text">
The people named in the rotation layers, once each. Empty when no layers are returned.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.pagerduty_schedule_id" type="Query filter">
The schedule id. Picks out one schedule. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.pagerduty_team_ids" type="Query filter, Access rule">
The owning teams' ids. Limits a query to one team's schedules. The teams also decide who can read the schedule. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="additional_metadata.pagerduty_teams" type="Query filter">
The owning teams' names. Limits a query to one team by name. Not searchable.
</ResponseField>

<Accordion title="Stored only, not searchable (1)">

<ResponseField name="schedule_id" type="Stored only">
The schedule id.
</ResponseField>

</Accordion>

### Escalation policies

- **One document:** One escalation policy.
- **Title:** The policy name, else its summary, else a generated title with its id.
- **Text:** The name and `Description`, one `Level` line per escalation level with its delay and targets, and a `Services` line with the services it is attached to. The title is searchable too.
- **Date:** None.
- **Link back:** The policy page in PagerDuty.

## How it fits into the graph

- Incidents use the Ticket class.
- Schedules use the Record class.
- Escalation policies use the Record class.

[Graph classes](/essentials/v2/connector-providers#graph-classes) explains each class.

**Connections PagerDuty creates**

- An incident to its escalation policy.
- An incident to the incident it was merged into.
- An escalation policy to each schedule it pages.
- No links to items in other tools.

**People and groups**

No one is attached as a person in the graph. Assignees, note authors and rotation members appear only in the text.

**Found in the text**

Incident text, including alert summaries, notes and status updates, and schedule and policy text. HydraDB identifies people, organizations, projects and other named things in this text. To steer what it looks for, use [custom instructions](/essentials/v2/connectors#4-custom-ingestion-instructions).

## Not synced

- Incidents of services you did not select.
- Incident notes and status updates when `fetch_incident_prose` is off.
- Dated on-call shifts, schedule overrides and rotation start times.
- Schedule members when PagerDuty returns no rotation layers.
- Services, teams and users as their own documents.
- Alert and notification records on their own. Only alerts inside an incident are searchable.
- Analytics, postmortems, response plays and on-call shift history.
